# D9.3.1 synthetic primary-custody runtime

Status: **approved at implementation commit `d960a03f4b8ac8a78d3f6b40ba909eb24b5f3442` for this synthetic, unactivated boundary only**. This implementation consumes the approved D9.0.1 and D9.3.0 contracts without changing them. Approval is not operational activation and does not authorize custody of real evidence, real bootstrap, evidence import, canonical writing, legal verification, public exposure, or production use. Exact-byte custody and technical auditability establish neither evidence acceptance nor officiality, legal identity, legal effect, or human legal verification. Recovery remains classification-only and fail-closed. The separate [D9.4.0 restriction/deletion contract proposal](d9-4-restriction-deletion-contracts.md) is awaiting approval; D9.4.1 is not implemented, and D9.5 backup and restore remains mandatory afterward.

## Boundary and audit result

The pre-implementation audit found no change was required to a frozen contract. D9.3.0 assigns primary-copy storage and durability to D9.3.1, reserves restriction/deletion for D9.4, and reserves backup/restore for D9.5. D9.1 does not implement recovery permits, and D9.3.1 is expressly forbidden from inventing that authority. Consequently, backup-dependent success histories, recovery-journal appends, and operational cross-store inventory snapshots are deliberately unreachable. The frozen 19-rule recovery matrix is evaluated only as classification logic; it executes and persists no recovery action in this tranche.

The runtime remains isolated from the 13 canonical Atlas tables. Tests use synthetic bytes, identities, clearances, manifests, and disposable protected directories. D9.3.1 stores physical bytes and technical audit facts only. Physical custody does not establish evidence acceptance, issuer identity, officiality, legal authority, legal effect, human review, or publication eligibility.

## CAS and durability

The CAS root is selected by trusted configuration and opened with `O_DIRECTORY | O_NOFOLLOW`; path and opened-descriptor identity, owner and permissions must agree before any provisioning. The root, objects directory, SHA directory, and all 256 fan-out names form a closed layout that is checked on construction and live operations. Partial prior initialization, missing or added namespaces, and substituted roots fail closed. All fan-out directories are pre-provisioned and synchronized before use. A prepared object is an owner-only, single-link regular file in a private temporary directory. The implementation reads an already-open source descriptor, checks byte layer, exact byte length and SHA-256, synchronizes file data, and rechecks file identity.

Publishing uses a hard-link no-replace primitive into the exact `objects/sha256/<prefix>/<digest>` target. `EEXIST` is accepted only after exact reopen, length, and hash verification. The final file and destination fan-out directory are synchronized before the private temporary name is removed and its directory synchronized; the result is then reopened and rehashed. Inventory also rehashes every filename-addressed object. Any error after temporary state exists latches the live CAS into `recovery_required`; later operations on that instance fail closed. A crash after link creation but before removal of the private temporary name is reported as `ambiguous_published_with_linked_temporary`; D9.3.1 performs no cleanup. Symlinks, hard-linked source/final objects in normal projections, special files, unsafe path parts, device/inode changes, fan-out substitution, and use of a closed root descriptor fail closed. No caller supplies a final filesystem path.

## IPC, capabilities, and descriptor lifetime

Every adapter request pins operation ID, nonce, sequence, sender/recipient bindings, runtime and binding generations, D9.3 profile, and canonical record digest. The operation ID is limited to 40 UTF-8 bytes, and request, response, supporting, receipt, semantic, and journal identities must match their closed deterministic formulas before any descriptor or CAS effect; this budget keeps every downstream stable code within the frozen 96-byte limit. The adapter also requires the exact admitted, durably persisted `operation_started` record before it opens staged bytes. The adapter obtains and consumes a fresh request/nonce-bound kernel peer proof rather than accepting caller-asserted peer facts. Source, preparation, and sealed-access bearer sidecars are 256-bit out-of-band values; they are not authority without their exact validated issuance/current leaf and context. Issuance requires a strictly earlier basis request, and every claim and transition must occur strictly after the current leaf, no later than the trusted operation time, and before expiry. These checks run before the associated effect. Source and sealed-access claims become terminal before their descriptor or CAS effect. The live capability resolver is deliberately ephemeral. On restart, the broker re-resolves the exact admitted handoff, bundle seal, synthetic clearance, primary receipt, custody projection fields, adapter stream and current operation-start root. Finalization capability issuance and transition records are reconstructed deterministically from immutable protected adapter exchanges and compared with every receipt/journal digest. The sealed issuance is also reconstructed exactly; its live consumption transition remains a stable digest continuity anchor across the protected integrity lifecycle because its trusted acceptance time is not independently persisted as a capability record in D9.3.0. Restart never treats that anchor alone as authority. Missing, expired, terminal, substituted, replayed, wrong-role, wrong-artifact, wrong-copy, wrong-backend, wrong-profile, wrong-operation, noncausal, and out-of-sequence claims fail closed.

`open_custody` re-resolves the exact receipt, custody projection, sealed scope and active synthetic clearance at claim and a fresh response-time boundary. The response requires an exact fresh projection evaluation and an unexpired sealed capability. It is integrity-only and its descriptor is retained behind a relay-owned branded sink: callers receive neither a descriptor map, a numeric file descriptor, nor an exported descriptor-consumer function. Only the matching integrity relay can consume the opaque one-shot result, and it derives lifecycle bundle, artifact, copy, backend, profile, actor, request, response, and capability facts from that hidden grant rather than caller claims. Failure after opening but before protected supporting acknowledgements closes the hidden descriptor. The relay uses `SOCK_SEQPACKET`, `SO_PEERCRED`/`SCM_CREDENTIALS`, `SCM_RIGHTS`, supplementary-group removal, a non-root UID/GID transition derived from the verified active synthetic service identity, `no_new_privs`, opened-executable rehashing, executable/process identity capture, and pidfd termination confirmation. The child closes the source descriptor inherited through `fork()` before receiving the sole custody descriptor through `SCM_RIGHTS`, then inventories its descriptors and requires exactly one alias of that custody object. The verifier result is durably acknowledged before termination is authorized. Any post-spawn exception terminates and reaps the receiver. Failure, death, or timeout requires recovery; the relay never grants processing access. A semantic append must be made later by a distinct live, descriptor-free coordinator after terminal acknowledgement.

Synthetic D9.4 clearance fixtures are used only to exercise the frozen `seal_custody_access` contract. No rights-decision process is implemented or authorized.

## Receipts, journal, and recovery

A primary receipt binds artifact identity/byte layer, copy, backend reference, bundle/seal, finalization response/capability facts, adapter build, no-replace disposition, and all four durability claims. Before the broker may persist it, the exact open/prepare/verify request-response pairs and publish request—seven distinct supporting records—must already have protected persistence acknowledgements. Canonical receipt-request bytes, receipt bytes, and response bytes are append-persisted and synchronized in order. Restart after the receipt effect is durable but before its response is returned reconstructs the exact response from the protected request and receipt. A supporting-record exchange is replayable only after its request, subject, and broker acknowledgement are all durable; an incomplete supporting exchange is recovery-required rather than reconstructed. Collisions fail closed.

Fixed journal namespaces hold primary receipts, receipt exchanges, supporting request/subject/response records, append requests/responses, journal-v2 events, and the reserved recovery-assessment namespace. Every protected filename must equal its canonical record code; a closed namespace/type matrix, exact non-null payload matrix, deterministic identity formula, and subject/request/acknowledgement bijection reject renamed, misplaced, overlong, or orphan records. Every broker mutation consumes a fresh request/time/nonce-bound synthetic Unix peer proof derived from kernel credentials and the active exact D9.0.1 binding. At trusted acceptance and persistence time the broker pins the active binding, runtime generation, identity generation, journal profile, producer role, executable, and exact unexpired bundle seal; caller-supplied attribution or backdated request time cannot mint broker authority. The seal must remain active at both broker boundaries before a new event is written. Exact post-crash acknowledgement reconstruction instead proves that the existing event was authorized at its immutable persistence time and adds no new semantic event. A new request must also fall within the bounded request-age window, while exact post-crash acknowledgement reconstruction may use its older persisted request. Request-only crash states are revalidated against the complete prerequisite graph and reconstructed head, reserve their exact operation and journal until their deterministic effect is completed, and reject competing requests. The broker enforces one journal per operation, one version, a registered history prefix, sequence `n + 1`, exact predecessor, stable bundle/target/authorization context, strictly increasing semantic and persistence time, and exact append-request/event/response reconstruction. Any unresolved post-persistence acknowledgement fault latches that broker instance into recovery-required. Reopening validates all records and cross-links, reconstructs exact heads, and returns only deeply frozen head projections. It validates the finalization and integrity adapter prefixes, fixed capability lifetimes, exact receipt/copy projection, and the one ordered descriptor-delivery → verifier-result → terminal lifecycle graph. An `available` one-shot descriptor without a complete `completed_verified` lifecycle is recovery-required after restart rather than silently reusable. Gaps, forks, mixed versions, duplicate identities, noncanonical bytes, or corruption fail closed. D9.3.1 requires the importer to originate `operation_started`, the custody adapter to originate `custody_object_durable`, and accepts only those two milestones of the unpermitted document-import prefix; bootstrap, recovery, backup-dependent events, candidate or promotion events, and completion are unreachable. Operational service IPC and protected deployment bindings remain activation prerequisites.

The protected journal root is either initialized once from empty or must already contain the exact complete namespace inventory; deletion or addition is never silently repaired. Root path/open identity, live owner and permissions, each selected namespace, and the private pending directory are revalidated before use.

The synthetic snapshot reader resolves one journal head and nine typed inventory test doubles sequentially, clones and deeply freezes the result, and marks it `synthetic_sequential_unactivated`, `atomic: false`. The protected recovery consumer rejects it. This makes the absence of a real cross-store atomic snapshot and D9.1 recovery-permit/lock authority fail closed instead of treating a caller assertion as protection. Separately, pure tests evaluate every branch of the frozen 19-rule matrix and map all 14 named crash boundaries; the CAS reports publication ambiguity but performs no cleanup. No recovery assessment is appended in D9.3.1. Every matrix result has `action_execution_code = none_classification_only`; no recovery action exists.

## Threat model, verification, and limits

Focused tests cover all six adapter operations; request-bound synthetic Unix peer credentials; generation/profile/build and trusted-time substitution; relay-private one-shot descriptor transfer; capability issue/verify/consume/replay and response-time expiry; final and ancestor symlink and hard-link rejection; CAS creation, reuse, collision, rehashed inventory, same-instance recovery latching, restart-visible crash states, and fail-closed startup after unresolved temporary state; the exact seven-record receipt graph, receipt/finalization cross-links, receipt-before-response ordering, durable receipt replay after response loss, and corrupted-receipt restart rejection; pending-file journal persistence and ambiguous-restart rejection; journal authentication, producer roles, exact response projections, exact replay, the two-milestone D9.3.1 cap, immutable heads, history prefix, fork/gap, and corruption; one-shot integrity result-before-termination, post-spawn cleanup, caller-metadata and executable substitution, and processing denial; all 19 recovery decisions, all 14 registered crash boundaries, deeply immutable non-atomic snapshot capture, and rejection of recovery persistence without authority; plus complete repository checks for frozen contracts, migrations, and empty Atlas data.

Additional adversarial coverage proves fresh-resolver restart reconstruction; event/request/persistence chronology; configured-root swaps; constructor and record-open descriptor cleanup; missing, added, or live-substituted storage namespaces; and content-address/fan-out corruption rejection.

The boundary cannot defend against a compromised kernel/root account, code already running as a protected service UID, storage hardware falsely acknowledging sync, rollback of an entire protected directory, operational clock compromise, or denial of service. The relay requires Linux, pidfds, Unix credentials, a C compiler, and the installed OpenSSL library and fails closed when unavailable. The synthetic profile uses a same-numeric non-root UID/GID because D9.0.1 binds only the Unix UID; an operational account/group allocation is required before activation. Operational service management, authenticated inter-service journal IPC, real credentials/bindings, protected deployment roots, cross-store snapshot/lock integration, rollback-resistant audit storage, alerting, D9.4 pre-promotion clearance/restriction/deletion, D9.5 backup/restore, and disaster recovery remain prerequisites.

Passing tests and approval establish only this synthetic, unactivated implementation boundary. Separate operational authorization and all documented prerequisites are required before any real bytes can be admitted.
